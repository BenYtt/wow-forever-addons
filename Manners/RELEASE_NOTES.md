- **The Manners macro on an action bar casts.** Clicking its slot, or pressing
  its key, did nothing on Classic Era: the game does not let a macro press a
  button that runs another macro, and that is what the old macro did. The
  macro now carries the cast itself: Manners writes what the prompt would cast
  into it, out of combat, whenever the prompt changes, so a press of the slot
  buffs whoever the prompt shows and hands your target back, as a press of the
  prompt does. It says no line: that goes out only with a press of the prompt,
  which looks one last time at whether the cast can land. In the last moments
  of a global cooldown, with nobody to buff, or while the macro window is open,
  it holds only its first line and a press says why; in a fight it keeps what
  it held when the fight began. An older Manners macro is updated for you. A
  `#showtooltip` you put on top stays; a macro you have changed in any other
  way is left as it is, and Manners says so once.
- **The prompt is made of something.** Glass is now a pane of cut glass with a
  metal bezel round the spell icon, which brightens under the pointer. Framed
  wears a rim like the game's tooltips. Toast's gold is hammered and worn, and
  the ring round its icon is a twist of rope.
- **A new look: Forge.** Forged iron and a riveted band, with the reason
  glowing in a slot beside the icon like a bar of heated steel. A favour
  arriving raises heat from the slot, and while it is owed the slot breathes
  three times and rests. A buff landing flashes the icon white-hot and throws
  sparks along the band; a refusal quenches the slot to steel with a puff of
  steam. That makes ten looks, each a card under *Look*.
- **The prompt arrives with light.** On Glass, Framed and Minimal, two thin
  rules of light draw out from the icon along the top and bottom edges as the
  prompt comes up, and the words follow a beat behind. A favour arriving runs a
  glint along them, in place of the band of light that used to cross the panel.
- **A landing is felt, a refusal is heard.** When a buff lands on Glass, Framed
  or Minimal, the panel's edge lights in the reason's colour (Minimal lights its
  two rules instead), a wave of light bursts from the icon across the panel, and
  the wash of colour fades away instead of cutting off. A refusal flashes the
  edge red, cracks the ground under the words, makes the top rule sputter,
  turns the icon grey and shakes the words. *Animations* on *Calm* keeps the
  fades and the grey icon and none of the movement.
- **Flash when someone buffs me** is no longer greyed out on Minimal with the
  icon hidden, since the glint answers it there.
